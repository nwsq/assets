# Royal Guard Handbook

This document serves as a guide to members of the Royal Guard on how to properly conduct their duties while in-game. Reading this document in its entirety is heavily recommended to provide an enjoyable and realistic gameplay experience for all, and to avoid committing any violations.

## Key Locations

### Wellington Barracks

This is where the Foot Guard division spawns, and it's the most quintessential building for the Royal Guards. Comprising four floors, it includes classrooms, lockers, and offices, with a car spawner on the ground floor. Remember to always close the gates when you have exited the premises.

### Buckingham Palace

Home to the Royal Family, and arguably the most important building within the UK - the Royal Guards are tasked with guarding it to their utmost ability. It is a central hub for tourists, citizens, and emergency services. It is absolutely imperative that the Royal Guards maintain order and safety within these grounds.

## Rank Structure

The Royal Guards are composed of both officers (command ranks) and other ranks (point-based ranks).

### Commissioned Officers

Commissioned Officers (COs), commonly referred to as just "Officers," are the command members of the Royal Guards. Qualified guards do not have authority over one another - a recruit and a warrant officer are equal in authority. Serious division staff and officers do carry authority within their chains of command. Officers should be addressed as "Sir" or "Ma'am" depending on their gender, and are to be saluted upon being greeted. To become an officer, you must pass the applications posted in vacancies.

**Rank & Description**

- **Brigadier** - The Brigadier is the officer commanding the Royal Guards and oversees Central Headquarters. They are responsible for setting the strategic agenda of the Royal Guards and dictating universal policy matters.
- **Colonel** - Colonels serve as the adjutants to the officer commanding the Royal Guards. They are responsible for liaising with regimental representatives and Central Headquarters in order to convey and achieve the strategic agenda set by headquarters. They are part of Central Headquarters. Colonels answer to the Brigadier.
- **Lieutenant Colonel** - Lieutenant Colonels serve as regimental commanders, and typically head up their regimental headquarters alongside their majors. They have jurisdiction over policy matters in their regiment, in compliance with wider policy. Lieutenant Colonels answer to Central Headquarters.
- **Major** - Majors are regimental second-in-commands, and generally act as company commanders as well as deputies to their regimental commander. They form the first rung of regimental headquarters and are responsible for acting as executives to their Lieutenant Colonel. Majors answer to their Lieutenant Colonel.
- **Captain** - Captains generally hold the role of platoon commander. They are typically in charge of a Lieutenant who holds the billet of platoon subcommander. They are responsible for ensuring their platoon performs to expectations, and that their platoon-based subordinates are performing effectively. They act as school and development trainers in the context of Training Company. Captains answer to their Major(s).
- **Lieutenant** - Lieutenants make up the majority of commissioned officers. In Training Company, they serve as a probationary rank for Officer Cadet School and development training. In serious divisions, they serve as platoon subcommanders who work closely with their Captain, non-commissioned officers, and other ranks in order to execute the strategic and tactical plans of the Royal Guards on the ground. Lieutenants answer to their Captains.
- **Second Lieutenant** - Second Lieutenants are newly commissioned officers, and answer to their Captain.
- **Officer Cadet** - Officer Cadets are officers in training and do not hold any authority beyond their scope of training. They are not commissioned.

### Other Ranks

The Other Ranks (ORs) make up the majority of the Royal Guards. Ranking up in the other ranks is done by earning points, either by playing the game or through attending trainings. These ranks are not entirely accurate to real life, and none of them hold any command.

**Rank & Description**

- **Warrant Officer 1** - WO1 is the most senior OR rank in the Royal Guards. In real life, they typically hold a command of "Regimental Sergeant Major" or "Garrison Sergeant Major." Obtained by getting 350 points total.
- **Warrant Officer 2** - WO2 is the second most senior OR rank in the Royal Guards. In real life, they typically hold a command of "Company Sergeant Major" or "Regimental Quartermaster Sergeant." Obtained by getting 250 points total.
- **Sergeant Major** - Sergeant Major is the third most senior OR rank in the Royal Guards. In real life, Sergeant Major is not a rank but rather an appointment that WO1s/WO2s are appointed to. Obtained by getting 150 points total.
- **Colour Sergeant** - The seventh rank in the Royal Guards. It has the same insignia as a Sergeant but with a crown added. Outside of the Infantry, this rank is known as "Staff Sergeant." Obtained by getting 100 points total.
- **Sergeant** - The sixth rank in the Royal Guards. It has the same number of chevrons as a Lance Sergeant, but they are coloured gold to indicate seniority. Obtained by getting 50 points total.
- **Lance Sergeant** - A rank equivalent to Corporal in the rest of the British Army. It has three chevrons in the foot guards, as opposed to two. Obtained by getting 30 points total.
- **Corporal** - Not a rank in the real-life foot guards (they just have "Lance Sergeant"); however, it is an additional rank here. Obtained by getting 10 points total.
- **Lance Corporal** - The third rank in the Royal Guards, and the first with a shoulder insignia. Lance Corporals in the foot guards have two chevrons, as opposed to one in the rest of the British Army. Obtained by getting 5 points total.
- **Guardsman** - The second rank in the Royal Guards. Obtained by getting 2 points. In the Household Cavalry, this rank is known as "Trooper."
- **Recruit** - The first rank you obtain when you first play in the Royal Guards.

## Divisions

The Royal Guards has three divisions designed for those who are interested in getting into the serious-roleplay side of things. They follow a company-like structure with an Officer Commanding (typically a Lieutenant Colonel), a Second-in-Command (typically a Major), Company Commanders (typically Captains), and multiple Platoon Commanders (ranking either Lieutenant or Second Lieutenant).

Members of the Royal Guards are permitted to be in a maximum of 2 divisions at any given time. This can be any combination of the three serious divisions.

### Training Company

The Training Company (TC) are the primary officers responsible for training new officer cadets in the Officer Cadet School and ensuring development training for Non-Commissioned Officers of serious divisions. They host exclusive qualifications such as the Guard Support Qualification. The company focuses on developing guards' skills and ensuring they're fully prepared for service. This regiment is officers-only.

## Trainings

### Guarding Qualification Course (GQC)

This training will cover performing guarding duties properly. This includes guard times, rules of engagement, area restrictions, duties of a sentry, and how to deal with incidents. Successfully passing this course spawns you at Wellington Barracks, and grants you zip ties and the ability to join a subdivision.

### Guard Support Qualification (GSQ)

This training covers how to engage with members of the public when not on guard duty, but in No. 8 Dress assisting guards who are on duty. Successfully completing this course will award you the GSQ qualification, access to No. 8 Dress in public servers, and access to first-aid equipment.

### Skill at Arms Training

This training is aimed at further improving the combative skill of members of the Royal Guards, by teaching the specifications and regulations of using the L85A2 while also engaging in practice combat situations.

### Drill & Turnout Training

This training teaches members of the Royal Guards how to properly execute drill instructions, both individually and as a unit. You will also obtain knowledge on how to carry out your duties in a parade.

### Military Knowledge Training

This training is aimed at educating all members of the Royal Guards on regimental history, basic game knowledge necessary to carry out your duties as a Guardsman, and the history of guarding.

## Equipment, Vehicles and Uniforms

### Equipment

As a Royal Guard, you are given various tools and equipment in order to carry out your duties. It is important you do not misuse your equipment, or you may be subject to disciplinary action from an officer. If you see anyone abusing their equipment, report them by opening a complaint ticket.

- **L85A2** - The standard issue rifle of the Royal Guards; a versatile and accurate rifle.
- **Union Jack Flags** - The national flag of the United Kingdom. It should be treated with respect and is used in formal events.

### Vehicles

When on public roads, Royal Guards must drive legally, following all speed limits and other traffic regulations. Make sure to lock your car after you have parked it in a designated parking spot, using "L".

You should only drive cars around with a proper reason, and only if you are in the No. 8 MTP uniform in assistance of guards on guard duties. You must have a Guard Support Certificate to be able to spawn any vehicles. You may only spawn a vehicle to transport yourself and two others to Buckingham Palace - otherwise, you may not spawn a vehicle. The maximum number of guardsmen you may transport per vehicle is the amount of seats the vehicle includes.

Those on duty as Support Guards (using their GSQ) may spawn a vehicle and use it to ferry personnel to and from Buckingham Palace, Wellington Barracks, or Horse Guards Parade.

- **Farlon Travano** - The standard issue car for the Royal Guards. Solid steering with eight seats (one driver seat, seven passenger seats). Comfortably fits a squad of Royal Guards. Spawnable by Lance Sergeants and above.
- **Mercurio Fleetmaster** - A larger car with more capacity than the Farlon Travano, to be used only when the extra capacity is necessary. It has 9 seats (one driver seat, eight passenger seats). Spawnable by Lance Sergeants who have passed a GSQ, and above.

### Uniforms

- **Full Dress** - The regular uniform that guards wear on duty.
- **No.2 Formal Dress** - Utilised for ceremonial and official events where a high level of formality is expected.
- **No.8 Dress** - Used in trainings and when assisting guards that are currently on duty. It consists of a Multi Terrain Pattern (MTP) uniform along with the regimental beret. No.8s may be worn by those with the GSQ certification, and only when both of the following are met:
  - There are 5 Royal Guards on duty.
  - There may only be a 1:5 ratio of Support Guardsmen (those actively using their GSQ) to Foot Guards - e.g. with 15 Guards online, there may only be 3 people actively using No.8s.
- **No.13 Dress** - A standard-issue British Army uniform to be worn only during Autumn and Winter. May be worn during daily work routines that do not include any sentry or major ceremonial duties, as well as during training and any non-serious parades or events. The cap must be worn for any act of formal duties in the No.13 dress, such as operating inside the palace grounds or Wellington Barracks. Guards in No.13s may also patrol Wellington Barracks, and may patrol inside the palace grounds without authorisation.
- **No.14 Dress** - A standard-issue British Army uniform to be worn only during Spring and Summer. May be worn during daily work routines that do not include any sentry or major ceremonial duties, as well as during training and any non-serious parades or events. The cap must be worn for any act of formal duties in the No.14 dress, such as operating inside the palace grounds or Wellington Barracks. Guards in No.14s may also patrol Wellington Barracks, and may patrol inside the palace grounds without authorisation.

## Regulations

There is a list of regulations that you are expected to follow. Failure to follow the regulations set forth can result in disciplinary action from an officer, ranging from a reminder to a suspension from playing on the Royal Guards team.

1. Royal Guards must always follow the game rules.
2. Royal Guards are expected to always be responsible for their actions.
3. Royal Guards are expected to maintain a sense of maturity when on-duty.
4. Royal Guards are to never neglect their duties.
5. Royal Guards are expected to maintain a sense of professionalism when conducting their duties.
6. Guardsmen wanting to go AFK should return to the menu.
   - If this is, for whatever reason, impossible, or the AFK period is under 5 minutes, they should head to the armoury/spawn to AFK.
7. Guardsmen are expected to abide by the law while on-duty.
8. Guardsmen should listen to orders given by those who have completed a Guard Qualification Certification, RMP, or a Commissioned Officer.
   - Where appropriate, Guardsmen should follow MPS CO or moderator orders.
9. Guardsmen are expected to abide by the Rules of Engagement, Area Access Regulations, and Standing Orders.
10. Failure to abide by the above will result in punishments, ranging from a reminder to a team suspension.

## Support Personnel Regulations

Guard Support Personnel are those who have passed the Guard Support Qualification, hosted by Lieutenants. Support Guardsmen are an integral role to the operation of the Royal Guard, and have permissions that extend to those of qualified Guardsmen.

The following regulations apply specifically to Guard Support Personnel, including regulations for the No.13 Dress and No.14 Dress:

1. Vehicles (Ford Tourneo, Mercedes Sprinter) must be parked in the ambassador entrance or inner courtyard. They are not permitted to be parked anywhere in the outer Palace.
2. Support staff may enter the interior of Buckingham Palace when:
   - They are requested by Guards who are already authorised to be there, or
   - They are tending to wounded personnel.
3. Support staff may exit the grounds of Buckingham Palace - but may not go past the perimeter road - in the following circumstances:
   - They are tending to wounded personnel.
   - They are assisting with dealing with a member of the public.
4. Support staff may transport Guardsmen of any rank or division in their vehicle. This must be exclusively from one post of duty to another.
5. Vehicles must be driven exclusively from Wellington Barracks to Buckingham Palace and back. You may only go to Horse Guards Parade, or take any other route, in these circumstances:
   - When dictated to do so by an officer of the Royal Guards.
   - If your vehicle is running low on petrol/gas and needs refuelling at the nearest petrol/gas station.
6. There may only be a 1:5 ratio of Support Staff to Guardsmen at any one time.
   - For every 5 Guardsmen, there may only be 1 person acting as a Support Guardsman. For example, 20 Guardsmen online would allow for 4 Support Staff to commence their duties.
7. Support staff cannot operate any vehicle other than their standard issued vans, regardless of authorisation.
8. Support staff operating in a No.13 Dress or No.14 Dress are still required to follow the Support Guard ratio.
   - The ratio may be disregarded when any non-serious events are hosted, or with explicit permission from a Lieutenant or above.
9. Support staff operating in a No.13 Dress or No.14 Dress do not focus on support duties, and are solely what could be classed as "staff" helping guards inside the palace act in their official duties.
10. Support staff operating in a No.13 Dress or No.14 Dress are permitted to remain within and patrol Wellington Barracks and the inside of the palace grounds. They may additionally patrol the gardens if instructed by a Second Lieutenant or above.
11. Support staff operating in a No.13 Dress or No.14 Dress are still required to follow the basic regulations of the Royal Guards, and are not permitted to use the vehicles provided to them. An exemption can only be given by a Captain or above.
12. Support Staff operating in a No.13 Dress or No.14 Dress should respect the seasonal uniform regulations. They cannot wear the No.13s during Summer and Spring, or the No.14s during Autumn and Winter. A Captain or above may provide exemptions.
13. Support Staff operating in a No.13 Dress or No.14 Dress cannot act in the official capacity of a Royal Military Police Constable, or an officer.
14. Support Staff operating in a No.13 Dress or No.14 Dress are permitted to expand their minor support duties to answering civilian enquiries, or enquiries from any Guardsman.
    - If required, they may exit the palace to answer said enquiries, as long as they remain within the area of the palace grounds.
15. Support staff operating in a No.13 Dress or No.14 Dress, when acting in their official duty in this uniform, are required to wear the cap provided to them. The uniform is only to be used in the following criteria:
    - The Guardsman is acting in a daily work routine that does not involve major ceremonial or sentry duty.
    - The Guardsman is partaking in a minor, non-serious ceremonial event, or any form of training if applicable.
    - The Guardsman is solely protecting the Barracks (Wellington), or assisting those within the palace grounds to enact their duty and provide assistance in protection.

## Rules of Engagement

The Rules of Engagement are vital in understanding when you are allowed to shoot, and when situations should be dealt with using less lethal force.

1. Civilians or emergency services too close to the fence without correct permissions (e.g. Close Protection) must be warned to get off the fence.
   - Guardsmen may point their service rifles at the climber after the second warning.
   - Guardsmen may fire a warning shot after the third warning.
   - Individuals who ignore warnings should be detained and pinned before being handed over to MPS.
2. Civilians or emergency services who are inside the fence without having correct permissions, but are less than 10 studs away from the gates, should be asked to leave ONCE.
   - If they do not comply, they should be detained and brought outside before being handed over to MPS.
   - If this is not possible and the trespasser has been given 3 warnings, they may be shot.
3. Civilians inside the fence and actively evading detention should be reported to MPS and, if possible, tased and then detained.
   - This may be obtained via help from Close Protection.
   - If this is not possible and the trespasser has been given 1 warning, they may be shot.
4. Civilians who are armed at any point within sight of the fence may be shot immediately, unless being dealt with by MPS.
5. Civilians who are armed inside the fence should be shot immediately.
6. Emergency services inside the fence without correct permissions should be reported to MPS immediately.
   - Close Protection or other authorised MPS divisions should either be with the King or in the Front Courtyard of the Palace. They are not allowed anywhere else.
   - Officers or those with clearance may detain them.

## Area Access Restrictions

1. No civilians, unless authorised by the Monarch, may enter the premises of Buckingham Palace, but may stand outside the fence.
   - They are only permitted to enter if they have the "Authorisation" tag above their username. If it is yellow, they need to be searched and should be forwarded to RMP or MPS as soon as possible.
2. No MPS, except Close Protection, authorised personnel, or other relevant divisions, may enter the premises of Buckingham Palace, but may stand outside the fence.
   - Authorised divisions should be in the Front Courtyard only, or with the King.
   - Those who are wandering should either be directed to the Front Courtyard or the King.
     - If they refuse to listen, they should be reported to a member of MPS Command through the ticket function located in the main server.
3. No Metropolitan Police Unit or other relevant divisions may enter the premises of Wellington Barracks, regardless of authorisation, but may stand outside the perimeter at any time, unless:
   - They have been authorised by a commissioned officer of the armed forces.
   - They are responding to an urgent threat and no armed forces personnel are available.
   - Same area restrictions are in place on the grounds of Saint James Palace.
4. Personnel may exit Buckingham Palace whilst escorting the Monarch as part of an Honour Guard entourage, subject to the rules below.
   - Any Guardsman may accompany the Monarch outside of Buckingham Palace if they have the "Authorised" tag above their head, given by the Monarch. Without this, they may not exit. Guards who exit the Palace must be with the Monarch and may not stray away.
   - Any member of the Royal Military Police may accompany the Monarch as an armed detail. Members of the military police do not need to be authorised. This is further expanded on internally.
   - Any member of the Life Guards may accompany the Monarch without needing an "Authorised" tag, as an Honour Guard. They must leave if the Monarch requests them to do so.
   - All other rules and standing orders must be obeyed during the course of this Honour Guard, excluding standing order 7.
5. Personnel may enter the Buckingham Palace interior whilst escorting the Monarch, subject to the rules below.
   - Any Guardsman may accompany the Monarch inside the Palace if they have the "Authorised" tag above their head, given by the Monarch. Without this, they may not enter.
   - Any member of the Royal Military Police may enter the interior of Buckingham Palace to effect an arrest, search, or preserve the King/Queen's peace.
   - Any member of the Grenadier Guards may station themselves inside the palace without needing an "Authorised" tag; however, they must leave if asked to do so by the Monarch or on-duty Life Guards.
   - The Life Guard, if necessary for ceremonial duties, may enter the palace. Equestrians are prohibited from entering the palace under all circumstances.
6. The Monarch has sole authority over who may or may not be allowed in; this should be respected at all times, unless concerns are raised which warrant moderation.
   - Any "unauthorised" personnel who cannot carry out their duties outside should make their way to Wellington Barracks and station there, or server change.
   - This rule ALSO applies to members of regiments. Any member of a regiment that happens to be unauthorised at any time must do the same as stated above.
7. No personnel except Grenadier Guards (combat attire), MPS Police Snipers, Commissioned Officers, or Specialist Firearms Officers of Met Operations 19 may be on the roof of Buckingham Palace.
   - The Monarch, the Royal Family, the Prime Minister, Private Secretary, and Royal Household are permitted on the roof.
   - Civilians and non-listed personnel are not permitted to be on the roof, regardless of the "Authorised" tag.
   - Any member of the Royal Military Police may have access to the roof to complete a patrol, looking for any unruly individuals.
   - Personnel carrying out the Honour Guard role or Close Protection Units are permitted on the roof only when deemed necessary to carry out their duty.
8. Personnel may enter the Buckingham Palace interior to respond to an ongoing heist, even if they do not have the Authorised tag.
   - Personnel must follow the standard rules of engagement with civilian trespassers.
   - All orders issued by Commissioned Officers and Royal Military Police must be obeyed.
   - Personnel must return to their posts when the heist concludes, as soon as possible. Guardsmen cannot linger around.

## Restricted Airspace

Multiple locations around the map are considered Restricted Airspace locations - these are no-fly zones for any aircraft. These locations are:

- Wellington Barracks
- Buckingham Palace

The only cases in which aircraft may enter these locations is with the authorisation of the Monarch or a Royal Guard Commissioned Officer (for Buckingham Palace), or a Royal Guard Commissioned Officer (for Wellington Barracks).

This applies to both civilian and Metropolitan Police Service aircraft. In the case of an airspace violation, a BOLO should be sent to INTER-SERVICE radio with the username of the subject, and the aircraft should be shot down.
