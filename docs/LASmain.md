# London Ambulance Service Handbook

This handbook is a guide to LAS personnel on how to carry out their duties.

## Rank Structure

The LAS is composed of both managers (command ranks) and other ranks (point-based ranks).

### Management Ranks

Managers are the command members who hold leadership positions in LAS.

- **Chief Executive Officer** - The lead executive of the entire London Ambulance Service. They are the highest authority in the service.
- **Deputy Chief Executive** - The Deputy Chief Executive(s) assist the Chief Executive in overseeing the service and help execute administrative decisions.
- **Director** - Directors are responsible for leading their assigned division. They have full control and authority of their division, with a member of Gold Command as their oversight, or are gold executives who assist within Gold Command.
- **Operations Manager** - Operations Managers are responsible for overseeing/running 2 or more units in a division, or in Training Managers & Student Manager School, which are primarily admin-based positions. They hold the position of second-in-command or divisional executive.
- **General Manager** - General Managers are usually assigned to run a unit; they are responsible for reporting to their divisional executive on the progress of their unit. General Managers form the core strength and voice of the management team. In Training Managers, Student Manager School, and Ombudsman, they are managers who train and lecture future personnel, or investigate misconduct in our serious divisions.
- **Junior Manager** - Junior Managers are newly appointed managers, new to the role. They are junior leaders in their role and position. Managers at this rank are still considered to be "in training."
- **Student Manager** - Student Managers hold no power or authority of a manager. They are in training until appointed by Gold Command and issued their manager certificate.

### Other Ranks

Other Ranks (ORs) make up the majority of LAS. Ranking up is done by earning points, either by playing the game or buying points. These ranks are not entirely accurate to real life, and none of them hold any command.

- **Team Leader** - The most senior OR rank in the London Ambulance Service. In real life, they typically hold command of a small dispatch of paramedics. Obtained by getting 350 points total.
- **Consultant** - In real life, a consultant is a specialist in their field. Obtained by getting 250 points total.
- **Doctor** - The eighth rank in the LAS. Obtained by getting 150 points total.
- **Senior Paramedic** - The seventh rank in LAS. Obtained by getting 100 points total.
- **Advanced Paramedic** - The sixth rank in LAS. Obtained by getting 50 points total.
- **Paramedic** - The fifth rank in LAS. Obtained by getting 30 points total.
- **Emergency Technician** - The fourth rank in LAS. Obtained by getting 15 points total.
- **Emergency Assistant** - The third rank in the LAS. Obtained by getting 5 points total.
- **Community Responder** - The second rank in the LAS. Obtained by getting 2 points total.
- **Student** - The first rank you obtain when you first play in the LAS.

## Equipment Description & Regulations

### Medical Bag

The standard issue equipment of the London Ambulance Service, used to heal wounded individuals.

### Bandage

Used to patch up wounds and stop bleeding. Works on all yellow-highlighted body parts.

### Defibrillator

Used to shock the heart, attempting to restart it if flatlined.

### Haemostat

Used to prevent major bleeding. Works on red-highlighted heads and torsos.

### Splint

A device used to support and protect injured limbs or wounds. Works on orange-highlighted body parts.

### Tourniquet

A device used to apply pressure to a limb or extremity to prevent major bleeding. Works on red-highlighted limbs.

### Vehicle Regulations

When on public roads, the London Ambulance Service must drive legally, following all speed limits and other traffic regulations, with exceptions such as responding to an emergency with your lights and sirens on. Make sure to lock your vehicle after you have parked it in a designated parking spot, using "L".

**Sirens**

Sirens are used to make your presence known to others using sound.

- Acceptable use: Responding to a medical emergency.
- Unacceptable use: Consistently keeping your sirens on for no reason, or using sirens as a means to avoid traffic or road laws when there is no ongoing emergency.

**Emergency Lighting System (ELS)**

Also known as ELS, this refers to your blue and red lights, situated around your vehicle to indicate your presence. Blue lights work similarly to sirens, making your presence known in the general area. Red lights, on the other hand, are only visible on the back of your vehicle - they warn oncoming vehicles that you are stationary.

- Acceptable use: When responding to an emergency, when on-scene at an emergency, or red lights when needing to be stationary.
- Unacceptable use: Randomly turning them on, or using them as a means to avoid traffic or road laws when there is no ongoing emergency.

Those on duty may spawn a vehicle and use it to ferry personnel to and from medical incidents and emergencies.

- **Fleece Jacket Dress** - The standard uniform for cold weather that medical personnel wear whilst on duty. Unlocked by successfully completing the Paramedic Qualification.

## Emergency Response Codes

It is the duty of responding personnel to prioritise and resource using the MPDS (Medical Priority Dispatch System) below, in order to maintain an appropriate response time that may be needed for other on-going or future medical emergencies. Note: this is not a 100% accurate representation of the actual MPDS.

- **Category 1** - Life-Threatening Illness or Injuries
- **Category 2** - Major Trauma or Injury
- **Category 3** - Moderate Trauma or Injuries
- **Category 4** - Moderate Bleeding
- **Category 5** - Mild Bleeding

### Appropriate use of ELS

The use of ELS depends on the CAT. For CAT 1 to 3, you are required to use your ELS and sirens. CAT 4 is optional and up to your own personal discretion. CAT 5 requires no ELS.

### Response Times

Each category should be dealt with within a specific time - for example, a CAT 1 should be responded to more quickly than a CAT 5.

- **Category 1/2** - 2-minute response time.
- **Category 3** - 3-minute response time.
- **Category 4** - 4-minute response time.
- **Category 5** - 5-minute response time.

## Safe Driving & Incident Attendance

It is paramount to the safety of yourself and other road users that you drive your service vehicle in an appropriate manner - avoiding dangerous driving, inappropriate parking at an incident scene, and over-attendance of an incident.

### General Service Vehicle Use

The following procedures are expected at all times when driving your service vehicle, even when not responding to an emergency (general use driving, returning to station, refuelling, etc.):

- Follow all road laws at all times. This includes, but is not limited to, speed limits and road signs (stop, give way, one way, etc.), and appropriate use of turn signals to indicate direction of travel.
- Avoid inappropriate use of the Emergency Light System (ELS) and/or sirens when not responding to an emergency.
- If you witness an incident during the course of general driving, or are yourself involved in an incident, stop in an appropriate place (covered below), turn on your ELS, and provide medical attention where necessary.

### High Speed Response Driving

There will be times when injured persons' need for medical attention depends on a fast but safe response time, requiring you to engage in high speed response driving. When this is required, the following procedures are expected at all times when responding to an incident:

- Ensure that the Emergency Light System (ELS) is activated and sirens are sounded throughout the journey.
- Ensure that when your speed exceeds the posted limit, it is always safe and not excessive.
- When approaching turns, junctions, or traffic lights, reduce your speed to reduce the risk of crashing or colliding with other vehicles.
  - Change the tone of your siren to the secondary sound to attract extra attention to your unexpected road manoeuvres.
  - Use your primary horn as a last resort to gain the attention of other individuals.
- Limit the use of sirens and excessive horn sounding once you have arrived at the scene of the emergency.

### Incident Attendance

When attending an incident scene, you need to ensure that you attend in an appropriate manner and do not contribute to excessive attendance at the scene. There are a number of key principles to follow, with additional principles applied to specific situations.

#### General Procedures

- Actively consider and aim to reduce the number of service vehicles attending a scene. Unless directed by senior management, the first-on-scene ambulance and 1 additional supporting service vehicle are all that is required for most incidents. Excessive service vehicles attending pose multiple issues, such as:
  - Impeding the regular flow of traffic.
  - Impacting access to a location or scene.
  - Creating an unsafe environment by having multiple moving vehicles.
- If you are not required to use your vehicle to carry out Safe Blocking Procedures (covered below), you must:
  - Park your service vehicle in an appropriate location, preferably in a designated parking spot or at the side of the road.
  - Never park parallel to another service vehicle, blocking access to that road. If a road needs to be closed, the Metropolitan Police Service will carry out their own defined procedures.
  - Unless you are the first on scene, you must park supporting vehicles in front of or behind the incident in a single file, ensuring you follow the first principle regarding excessive attendance.
- You must always park your vehicle facing the direction of travel, and utilise the red ELS if your service vehicle is stationary as opposed to parked.
- You must move your service vehicle away from a scene, or to an appropriate parking area, once an incident has concluded. You must not park your service vehicle in contradiction to any posted parking regulations or road rules outside of emergency situations. You may temporarily ignore these signs to gain access to an incident scene; however, you must obey them immediately upon the incident's conclusion.

#### Vehicles & Road Incidents

If attending a vehicle-related incident, or a similar incident located on or near a road not involving other vehicles, you will be expected to consider the following principles in addition to the general procedures:

- Follow road traffic rules at all times, and use the Emergency Lighting System (ELS) to warn others of your presence, ensuring where possible you park your service vehicle in the direction of traffic and activate the rear-facing red ELS to warn other road users that your vehicle is stationary.
- Do not impede the flow of traffic unless absolutely necessary to protect the scene, if located on or near the road. If required, work with the Metropolitan Police Service to create blocks or manage traffic.
- Utilise the Safe Blocking Procedures where necessary.

#### Isolated & Structural Incidents

If attending incidents in isolated areas or within structures away from the road:

- Park as close and as safely as possible to the main entrance of the location or near the scene, making sure not to restrict or block access for other emergency services.
- Utilise any nearby designated parking, unless responding to critical incidents.

#### Safe Blocking Procedures

Safe Blocking Procedures are where you, or another emergency service member, use your vehicle to protect a scene from the risk of secondary collisions, additional traffic incidents, or additional casualties. These procedures are mainly used for vehicle or road-related incidents, such as persons hit by a vehicle, single-vehicle crashes, or multiple-car collisions.

**First on Scene**

If you are the first service vehicle to attend an incident, you will use your service vehicle to shield the incident. Usually this role is for the Metropolitan Police Service; if the situation warrants it, such as you being the only responding service vehicle, you may employ your vehicle to take this position.

Key principles:

- If you are first on scene, it is recommended that you use your service vehicle to shield the incident by approaching from the rear and maintaining a safe distance of 5-10 metres from the incident.
- If you are in a single lane, use your service vehicle to protect the scene by positioning it directly behind, or up to 50% offset to one side of, the incident. This is so approaching vehicles do not collide with the scene and can clearly see your service vehicle and ELS.
- Ensure that your parking brake is on, to avoid a colliding vehicle shunting yours into the scene and causing more danger (observing the 10m distance as a buffer).

**Supporting Units**

If you are attending a scene to support the first-on-scene vehicle, you must follow these key procedures:

- The first responding vehicle should park at the front of the incident - the opposite side from the first responder - maintaining the same 5-10 metre gap from the incident.
  - Keep your service vehicle as close to the side of the road as possible, or utilise a 45-degree angle away from traffic to deflect oncoming vehicles, if on a multi-lane road.
- The second supporting vehicle, or the heavier vehicle, should park behind the first respondent to provide the same deflection protection to the loading zone of the first-on-scene vehicle.
- All other supporting service vehicles, if required and authorised to attend, must park to one side of the road in a single file behind the first-on-scene vehicle and any supporting service vehicles - i.e. behind the incident.

## Emergency Driving Exemptions

There are a number of exemptions within the Road Traffic Regulations Act 1984 and other such statutes that apply to emergency response drivers whilst operating under the Ambulance Service. These can often be contradictory to the ordinary rules stated within the Act.

The following exemptions (and non-exemptions) have been directly extracted from the South Central Ambulance Service NHS Foundation Trust Driving and Care of Trust Vehicles 2019 guidance, but condensed for easier understanding. This has been reviewed by Gold Command and also applies to our London Ambulance Service.

### Speed limits

Ambulance drivers may exceed statutory speed limits only if necessary for emergency purposes (e.g. responding to a call, or if a patient's condition justifies it). Safety is the overriding priority: always drive at a speed that allows you to see, plan, and react to hazards safely.

Under the Road Traffic Regulation Act 1984, Section 87, statutory speed limits do not apply to a vehicle being used for Fire Brigade, Ambulance, or Police purposes, if observing the limit would be likely to hinder the use of that vehicle for the purpose it is being used for on that occasion. This does not allow dangerous, reckless, or careless driving - police can still prosecute for excessive speed.

### Traffic control signals

When using ELS lights and responding, red lights must be treated as GIVE WAY signs. Only proceed when you are certain it is safe and all road users have reacted appropriately.

Sirens and lights do not give right of way - they only serve as a warning. Never use sirens to force vehicles across a stop line. Temporary traffic lights and pedestrian crossings carry the same rules: proceed only when absolutely safe. There is no exemption whatsoever from "give way" afforded to emergency service vehicles.

The general public are advised to assist emergency service vehicles engaged on an emergency call, by reacting and allowing safe passage. The overriding responsibility for safety rests with the emergency service driver at all times. Approach every red traffic signal at reduced speed, and only enter the junction if you are certain it is safe and won't endanger others. If in doubt, STOP.

### Temporary Signals & Pedestrian Crossings

Temporary lights have the same legal standing as fixed lights. Proceed only if safe, even if waved through. At pedestrian crossings, do not proceed until pedestrians have clearly seen and responded to you. As a driver, you must always be prepared to give way to other road users who have legal priority.

### Bus Lanes

May be used only during emergencies, unless local agreements state otherwise. You must always follow the direction of flow and take great care entering/exiting.

### Clearways

LAS vehicles are permitted to stop and park on clearways, subject to it being necessary to carry out essential duties that could not otherwise be done. No driver is exempt from leaving the vehicle in a dangerous position - it is their responsibility to ensure the vehicle is not left in a manner, position, or circumstance likely to present a danger to other road users. All precautionary means (e.g. blue lights, rear red flashing lights, flashing beacons, or hazard warning lights) should be considered when stopping in potentially hazardous situations.

### Parking within restricted areas surrounding pedestrian crossings (Zebra, Pelican, Puffin and Toucan)

When attending a scene, ambulance vehicles are permitted to park within the controlled area and on the crossing, subject to the vehicle not remaining longer than necessary, and/or it not being possible to park more effectively elsewhere. This exemption is to allow ambulance personnel to deal with an emergency, and would not be justified for routine non-emergency duties. Justification must be absolute, as dangerous parking could be a strong consideration in these instances.

### Double White / Double Yellow lines

Parking at or near double white or yellow lines is permitted at a scene, subject to the same conditions as above.

### Non-Exemptions

There are no other exemptions for the driver of an emergency vehicle except those described above, even when responding to an emergency call. Examples of actions where a legal exemption does not exist:

- Crossing or straddling a solid white line nearest to you in the centre of the road (other than those occasions listed in Highway Code rule 129).
- Dangerous driving.
- Dangerous parking.
- Failing to obey a "NO ENTRY" sign, unless permitted to by a qualified MPS officer.
- Failing to obey a "ONE WAY TRAFFIC" sign.
- Failing to stop if involved in a road traffic incident (applying safeguarding is an allowable exemption, e.g. civilians are targeting you or you are in imminent danger).

Drivers must exercise common sense at all times. Any driving that compromises the safety of other road users, or breaches traffic laws, is prohibited.

## Medical Ethics

Medical Ethics are based on set values that all LAS personnel should refer to and uphold when performing their duties. These are basic principles that are an essential component of patient care.

### Duty of Care

The Duty of Care as LAS personnel is paramount in everything you do. It's what binds the prioritisation, ethical wellbeing, and welfare of every individual. It promotes good practice and compliance across the division, and should be followed under every circumstance. The Duty of Care is defined by multiple questions you must ask yourself:

"Does it protect patients' safety? Is it effective? Is it responsive to their needs? Is it discrimination-free? Does it uphold the safeguarding of others?"

For example, if a criminal who is a threat to life is currently downed, ensure it's viable to give them medical treatment by prioritising the safeguarding of others - for instance, having an officer present, ready to detain them, before you begin treatment.

### Safeguarding

Safeguarding refers to the measures taken to protect vulnerable groups, including yourself, from harm and abuse. It aims to ensure good health and wellbeing for all individuals, as well as preserving their human rights. It's important to apply safeguarding in everything you do at all times whilst on-duty - it's fundamental to high-quality and unbiased medical and social care.

### Do Not Resuscitate (DNR)

DNR, or DNACPR, stands for "do not attempt cardiopulmonary resuscitation." This means that if an individual is downed, they have the right to refuse medical care from you. However, this ethic does not apply when dealing with suspects currently being detained or pursued by a Metropolitan Police officer.

## Operations Scenarios

As a Paramedic, you must be aware of the various common scenarios you may come across while on duty - such as firearms incidents, traffic collisions, and other trauma events. There are protocols for responding to these scenarios.

### Parades and Inspections (in public servers)

These events are not to be attended by paramedics on-duty, unless you are specifically requested for an emergency.

### Parades and Inspections (in joint shifts/joint events)

Qualified Paramedics may attend such events at their discretion. These events are conducted internally by serious divisions and COs.

### Firearm Incidents

You may only respond to these incidents once you are certain the scene is completely safe. Never compromise your own safety in order to provide medical assistance.

### Hostage Situations

Paramedics should only attend hostage situations when explicitly requested. If you are present at an incident:

- Do not attempt to access the hostage location.
- Entry is strictly prohibited unless you receive direct authorisation from MPS, preferably MO19 if they are on-scene.

### Protests

Peaceful protests rarely require paramedics' attendance. You may be called to treat minor injuries if required. Once an incident is resolved:

- You must leave the scene to allow MPS to continue handling the situation.
- Do not involve yourself in any protest unless specifically requested/authorised by MO7, Chief Officers, or Supervisors.

### Checkpoints

Paramedics must not loiter around checkpoints - continue your normal duties unless medical assistance is requested.

- You may only attend a checkpoint if requested by an MO8 Chief Officer.
- You must not remain on scene unless authorised by both an MO8 Chief Officer and an LAS Manager (if present).
- If specialist LAS members are active in the server, you must not attend the checkpoint incident and instead leave it to them.

Members of the Critical Care Team & Hazardous Area Response Team are permitted to attend these incidents, provided they adhere to their internal regulations.
